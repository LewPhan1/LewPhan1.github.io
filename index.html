About

Who am I?

I’m a research scientist with a particular interest in understanding pharmacometrics through mechanistic modelling. My work has taken me from agent-based models and high-performance computing during my PhD to ODE-based modelling and quantitative experiments in my current postdoctoral research at the University of Sheffield. Along the way, an internship at Certara Simcyp sparked a growing interest in PBPK and pharmacometrics, which I’ve continued to pursue through certified training, scientific meetings, and my own modelling projects. This blog is partly a record of that journey as I continue developing my skills in PBPK, DMPK, AI, and model-informed drug development.

Selected work

Models, pipelines, and the code behind them

Agentic PBPK Workflow

A multi-agent pipeline that builds a 4-compartment oral PBPK model for midazolam straight from literature and label data — resolving the drug, searching the literature, extracting parameters, checking evidence quality, building and running the model, then critiquing and writing up the result. Pulls live from PubChem, Europe PMC, and openFDA.

Python Agent orchestration Artificial intelligence

APAP PBPK Reproduction

A 7-compartment perfusion-limited PBPK model reproducing Figure 4 of Sluka et al. (2016, PLOS ONE) for acetaminophen pharmacokinetics — parameter fitting, sensitivity analysis, and non-compartmental analysis (NCA) metrics, cross-checked against a BioModels SBML reference.

Python scipy · solve_ivp (LSODA) NCA

MTM1 / Snx17 Membrane Kinetics

Modeling lipid-phosphatase reaction kinetics on membranes — PI3P catalysis, probe binding, and cargo interactions — porting an ODE simulation from Python to Julia for performance, alongside an ongoing evaluation of agent-based modeling frameworks for the spatial version of the system.

Julia DifferentialEquations.jl (Rodas5) Agent-based modeling

Postdoctoral researcher

University of Sheffield · April 2024 – Present

In my current postdoctoral research, I use mechanistic ODE models to understand membrane lipid-identity switching and interpret quantitative experimental data. I also developed an in vitro phosphatase system using planar lipid bilayers and microscopy, generating controlled datasets to parameterise, test, and refine these models.

PhD in mechanistic systems biology

University of Sheffield · October 2018 – December 2023

During my PhD in computational systems biology, I combined experimental biochemistry with mechanistic modelling to study the spatial organisation and competitive regulation of endocytosis. I developed a de novo agent-based model in C/C++, parallelised it on a GPU, and used it to generate hypotheses and guide in vitro experiments. A key part of the project was iterating between modelling and experiment, using each to inform and refine the other.

Internship in pharmacometrics

Certara Simcyp · October 2020 – January 2021

My internship at Certara Simcyp was where my interest in pharmacokinetics and PBPK really began to take shape. I worked on Python packages and API workflows for preclinical toxicokinetic and PK data, analysed exposure and concentration–toxicity relationships, and gained first-hand insight into how mechanistic PK models are developed and applied through discussions with the Simcyp team.
